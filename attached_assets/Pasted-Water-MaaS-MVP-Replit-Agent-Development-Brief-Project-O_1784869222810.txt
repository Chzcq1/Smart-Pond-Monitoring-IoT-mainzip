Water-MaaS MVP – Replit Agent Development Brief

Project Overview

This project is not a final production system.

It is an MVP (Minimum Viable Product) created for business validation and project presentation.

The objective is to demonstrate how a Water Monitoring-as-a-Service (Water-MaaS) platform would work before any physical hardware prototype is deployed.

At this stage, there are:

* No ESP8266 devices
* No real sensors
* No actual industrial data

All monitoring values will be generated from simulated datasets to emulate a real industrial wastewater monitoring environment.

The website itself will become the presentation platform instead of traditional PowerPoint slides.

⸻

Background

Many SME factories cannot afford industrial online water quality monitoring systems because:

* High upfront equipment cost (CapEx)
* Complex maintenance
* Sensor calibration difficulties
* No in-house environmental engineer
* Lack of real-time monitoring

Our business proposes an alternative:

Water Monitoring-as-a-Service (Water-MaaS)

Instead of selling expensive monitoring equipment, we provide:

* Monthly subscription
* Cloud dashboard
* Automatic reporting
* LINE OA notifications
* Scheduled maintenance reminders
* Calibration service (future phase)
* Estimated water quality trends

This MVP demonstrates that business concept.

⸻

Important Scope

This MVP DOES NOT claim to measure laboratory BOD directly.

The platform only demonstrates:

* Water quality monitoring
* Data visualization
* Estimated BOD trend (simulated)
* Alert management
* Reporting workflow

Future versions may integrate mathematical surrogate models and real IoT devices.

⸻

MVP Goal

Create a modern SaaS dashboard capable of demonstrating:

* Multi-factory support
* Multiple monitoring stations (machines/tanks)
* Live sensor dashboard
* Historical graphs
* PDF report generation
* Maintenance scheduling
* Alert system
* Expandable architecture

The project should be designed so real ESP8266 devices and APIs can replace simulated data in the future without redesigning the frontend.

* Online Devices

* Status

Simulated Data

Since no hardware exists yet:

Generate realistic sensor values.

Avoid completely random jumps.

Examples:

Temperature changes slowly.

pH remains relatively stable.

DO gradually decreases under abnormal conditions.

Estimated BOD increases gradually during simulated failures.

Turbidity fluctuates realistically.

The simulation should look believable.
